# SOP — BASE Structured Analytical Exposition v0.1

## 1. Objective

The objective of BASE is to transform a completed BSI analysis into an integrated analytical presentation while preserving analytical meaning, provenance, uncertainty, scope, relations, and epistemic boundaries.

BASE is an exposition procedure, not a new analytical procedure.

---

## 2. Mandatory Boundary

BASE begins only after a BSI analysis has been completed.

INPUT:

Completed BSI Analysis

BASE must not:

- perform new BSI analysis;
- redefine BSI;
- change BSI criteria;
- change BSI weights;
- perform new BSI scoring;
- independently revalidate BSI;
- introduce new evidence;
- upgrade epistemic status;
- erase analytical provenance.

If any of these become necessary, execution must stop and escalate to BSI.

---

## 3. Execution Pipeline

The BASE execution pipeline is:

1. Analytical Inventory
2. Provenance Mapping
3. Analytical Hierarchization
4. Relation Mapping
5. Gap and Conflict Placement
6. Synthetic Linking
7. Narrative Architecture
8. Analytical Exposition
9. Epistemic Calibration
10. Reading Optimization
11. Presentation Integrity Check

---

## 4. Step 1 — Analytical Inventory

Identify the complete set of materially relevant analytical elements contained in the completed BSI analysis.

Inventory should include, where applicable:

- central findings;
- secondary findings;
- supporting findings;
- interpretations;
- structural implications;
- evidence relationships;
- uncertainty;
- qualifications;
- scope boundaries;
- counterpoints;
- unresolved conflicts;
- limitations.

Do not begin narrative compression before the analytical inventory is sufficiently complete.

---

## 5. Step 2 — Provenance Mapping

Map important presentation elements to their analytical origins.

For each important element, determine:

- what BSI finding supports it;
- what analytical basis supports that finding;
- what evidence or source context underlies it;
- what epistemic qualification applies.

Provenance may remain implicit in the final prose when recoverable, but must not be destroyed.

---

## 6. Step 3 — Analytical Hierarchization

Organize analytical elements according to structural importance.

Distinguish:

- central findings;
- major structural relations;
- supporting findings;
- interpretations;
- qualifications;
- counterpoints;
- trace-level material.

Do not use textual length as a substitute for analytical importance.

Do not delete material merely because it is secondary.

---

## 7. Step 4 — Relation Mapping

Identify the materially relevant relationships among analytical elements.

Check for:

- causal relations;
- conditional relations;
- temporal relations;
- dependency relations;
- contrasts;
- evidence-to-finding relations;
- finding-to-interpretation relations;
- uncertainty;
- scope;
- competing interpretations.

The final exposition must preserve these relationships.

---

## 8. Step 5 — Gap and Conflict Placement

Identify analytical gaps, uncertainties, unresolved conflicts, and counterpoints.

Place them where they are most relevant to the claims they qualify.

Do not automatically move all limitations to the end.

Do not suppress conflicts for narrative simplicity.

**Counterpoint Preservation Guard:** BASE must preserve competing interpretations, contradictions, unresolved conflicts, and relevant counterevidence when they are materially relevant to the completed BSI analysis. Narrative simplification must not remove or conceal such counterpoints.

---

## 9. Step 6 — Synthetic Linking
**No New Substantive Relation Guard:** Linking may reorganize or expose an existing relation, but may not create a new causal, explanatory, predictive, theoretical, factual, or otherwise substantive relation.

**Structural Implication Guard:** Any structural implication expressed through linking must already be present in the completed BSI analysis or be a logically equivalent re-expression of it. If establishing the implication requires new analysis, stop BASE execution and escalate to BSI.


Where existing BSI findings are distributed across the analysis, BASE may construct explicit links between them.

Synthetic linking is permitted only when the relationship is recoverable from existing analytical material.

It must not create:

- new evidence;
- new causal mechanisms;
- new explanations;
- new predictions;
- new theoretical claims;
- new analytical findings.

---

## 10. Emergent Presentation Insight

BASE may reveal an implicit structural relationship that becomes visible when completed BSI findings are reorganized.

Such an insight is valid only if it is a logically recoverable presentation-level synthesis.

It must not exceed the epistemic content of the underlying BSI analysis.

---

## 11. Escalation Rule

If a required presentation statement cannot be supported by the completed BSI analysis without introducing new analytical work, stop BASE execution for that element.

Use:

BASE
→ Analytical insufficiency
→ BSI
→ Updated Completed BSI Analysis
→ BASE resumes

Never silently fill an analytical gap.

---

## 12. Step 7 — Narrative Architecture

Construct the narrative around the analytical structure rather than source chronology.

A suitable sequence may include:

1. Central analytical structure
2. Major components
3. Relationships among components
4. Supporting findings
5. Interpretations
6. Qualifications and uncertainty
7. Counterpoints and unresolved issues
8. Structural implications
9. Trace/support layer

The exact order may vary when analytical visibility requires it.

---

## 13. Step 8 — Analytical Exposition

Convert the organized analytical structure into coherent prose.

The exposition should:

- make hierarchy visible;
- make relations understandable;
- maintain provenance;
- preserve epistemic status;
- avoid unnecessary repetition;
- preserve important qualifications;
- retain counterpoints;
- distinguish evidence from interpretation.

The objective is to expose the analysis, not to create a new analysis.

---

## 14. Step 9 — Epistemic Calibration

Review every materially important statement for epistemic fidelity.

Check that:

- possible has not become established;
- suggestive has not become demonstrated;
- associated has not become causal;
- limited has not become general;
- uncertain has not become certain;
- hypothesis has not become finding;
- interpretation has not become observation.

Where necessary, restore the original qualification.

---

## 15. Step 10 — Reading Optimization

Optimize the presentation for analytical comprehension.

Check:

- whether the central structure is immediately visible;
- whether the hierarchy is clear;
- whether relations are easy to follow;
- whether qualifications occur near relevant claims;
- whether important counterpoints remain visible;
- whether repetition can be reduced without information loss;
- whether the narrative follows analytical rather than merely textual logic.

Do not optimize readability by reducing epistemic content.

---

## 16. Main Narrative and Trace Layer

Where appropriate, produce:

### Main Narrative

The integrated analytical presentation intended for normal reading.

### Trace Layer

The supporting analytical details necessary to recover:

- provenance;
- qualifications;
- evidence relationships;
- conflicts;
- unresolved issues;
- analytical support.

The main narrative may be concise.

The trace layer must preserve recoverability.

**Trace Layer Requirement:** The Trace Layer is required whenever the Main Narrative alone would make provenance, qualifications, evidence relationships, conflicts, unresolved issues, or analytical support unrecoverable.

---

## 17. Step 11 — Presentation Integrity Check

Before completion, compare the BASE presentation against the completed BSI analysis.

Check:

### Content Fidelity
Have materially relevant findings been preserved?

### Relation Fidelity
Have materially relevant relationships been preserved?

### Epistemic Fidelity
Has uncertainty or qualification been preserved?

### Scope Fidelity
Has any claim been broadened beyond its original scope?

### Provenance Fidelity
Can important claims still be traced to their analytical basis?

### Counterpoint Fidelity
Have relevant competing interpretations and conflicts remained visible?

### Non-Interference
Has BASE avoided altering BSI criteria, weights, scoring, or definitions?

### No Hidden Analysis
Has BASE avoided introducing genuinely new analytical inference?

---

## 18. Completion Conditions

BASE may be considered complete only when:

1. the completed BSI analysis has been adequately inventoried;
2. important provenance is recoverable;
3. analytical hierarchy is visible;
4. materially relevant relations are preserved;
5. uncertainty and qualifications are preserved;
6. counterpoints remain visible;
7. scope has not expanded;
8. no hidden epistemic upgrade has occurred;
9. no new analytical inference has been silently introduced;
10. the final presentation improves analytical visibility.

---

## 19. Failure Conditions

BASE execution fails when:

- new analysis is silently introduced;
- new evidence is introduced;
- BSI criteria are changed;
- BSI weights are changed;
- BSI scoring is performed or altered;
- uncertainty is removed;
- scope is expanded;
- counterpoints are deleted;
- provenance becomes unrecoverable;
- causal language is strengthened without analytical basis;
- readability is achieved through epistemic loss.

---

## 20. Quality Test

A BASE presentation should pass the following conceptual test:

### Visibility
Can the reader see the central analytical structure?

### Coherence
Can the reader follow the relationships among major findings?

### Traceability
Can important claims be traced back to their analytical basis?

### Calibration
Does the language preserve the original epistemic status?

### Completeness
Are materially relevant findings, qualifications, and counterpoints retained?

### Non-Interference
Has presentation changed only visibility and organization, not analytical meaning?

---

## 21. Core Rules

1. Present the analysis; do not replace it.
2. Organize the findings; do not redefine them.
3. Clarify relations; do not invent them.
4. Compress language; do not compress epistemic structure.
5. Improve readability; do not weaken qualification.
6. Preserve provenance.
7. Preserve uncertainty.
8. Preserve scope.
9. Preserve counterpoints.
10. Escalate analytical insufficiency to BSI.
11. Never silently upgrade an epistemic claim.
12. Never allow narrative convenience to override analytical fidelity.

---

## 22. Final BASE Contract

INPUT:
Completed BSI Analysis

PROCESS:
Organize, Hierarchize, Link, Synthesize, Expose, Calibrate, Optimize, Audit

OUTPUT:
Integrated Analytical Presentation

PROHIBITED:
New Analysis, New Evidence, New Scoring, New BSI Criteria, Independent Revalidation of BSI, Epistemic Upgrading, Provenance Loss

---

## 23. Final Statement

BASE reduces the effort required to understand an analysis without reducing the analytical effort required to understand the subject.

BASE changes how completed BSI analysis is presented.

BASE does not change what completed BSI analysis means.
