# BASE — Analytical Exposition Architecture v0.2

## 1. Purpose

BASE is a post-analytical exposition architecture for presenting a completed BSI analysis with maximum analytical visibility, structural clarity, traceability, and reading efficiency while preserving the analytical and epistemic integrity of the underlying analysis.

BASE exists to expose the structure already produced by BSI, not to replace, redefine, score, or independently revalidate it.

---

## 2. Architectural Position

BASE operates after completion of BSI analysis.

The architectural relationship is:

BSI → Completed Analytical Structure → BASE → Integrated Analytical Presentation

BSI produces the analytical structure.

BASE organizes and exposes that structure for human comprehension.

BASE does not become an additional BSI analytical layer.

---

## 3. Core Definition

BASE is a post-analytical exposition layer that reorganizes the visibility, hierarchy, linkage, and narrative presentation of a completed BSI analysis while preserving its analytical and epistemic integrity.

---

## 4. Analytical Visibility

The primary objective of BASE is analytical visibility.

BASE must make visible:

- what the analysis found;
- how findings relate to one another;
- what supports each important finding;
- what interpretations follow from those findings;
- what remains uncertain;
- what scope limitations apply;
- what competing interpretations remain;
- and where the boundaries of the analysis lie.

BASE optimizes the reader's ability to see the analysis.

It does not reduce the analytical effort required to understand the underlying subject.

---

## 5. BSI–BASE Boundary

BSI is responsible for analysis.

BASE is responsible for exposition.

BSI determines the analytical content.

BASE determines how that completed analytical content is organized and presented.

If producing an adequate exposition requires genuinely new analysis, BASE must stop and escalate the requirement back to BSI.

---

## 6. Protected BSI Core

BASE must not alter:

- BSI definitions;
- BSI principles;
- BSI dimensions;
- BSI criteria;
- BSI weights;
- BSI scoring;
- BSI epistemic boundaries;
- BSI findings;
- BSI qualifications;
- BSI evidence relationships.

BASE may reorganize their presentation but may not redefine their meaning.

---

## 7. Analytical Visibility Model

BASE should make the following hierarchy visible where applicable:

1. Central findings
2. Structural relations among findings
3. Supporting findings
4. Interpretive consequences
5. Qualifications and uncertainty
6. Counterpoints and competing interpretations
7. Evidence and provenance
8. Trace-level analytical detail

The hierarchy is organizational.

It must never become a mechanism for deleting analytically relevant information.

---

## 8. Analytical Hierarchy

BASE should distinguish between:

- central findings;
- secondary findings;
- supporting observations;
- structural relationships;
- interpretive statements;
- qualifications;
- unresolved issues;
- evidence provenance.

Hierarchy must reflect analytical importance, not merely textual prominence.

A short statement may be structurally more important than a long section.

---

## 9. Analytical Compression

BASE may compress language.

BASE may compress repetition.

BASE may consolidate equivalent statements.

BASE may reorganize distributed material into a clearer analytical structure.

BASE must not compress away epistemically relevant distinctions.

Three forms of compression must therefore be distinguished:

### 9.1 Linguistic Compression

Reducing wording while preserving meaning.

### 9.2 Structural Compression

Combining analytically equivalent or closely related elements while preserving their relations.

### 9.3 Epistemic Compression

Removing uncertainty, qualification, scope, provenance, disagreement, or evidentiary distinctions.

Epistemic compression is prohibited.

---

## 10. Provenance Mapping

Every materially important BASE claim must remain traceable to its analytical basis.

The provenance chain should remain conceptually recoverable:

BASE statement → BSI finding → analytical basis → evidence / source context → epistemic boundary

BASE does not need to display the entire chain in every sentence, but it must not sever the chain.

---

## 11. Relation Preservation

BASE must preserve materially relevant relations, including where applicable:

- causal;
- conditional;
- temporal;
- dependency;
- contrastive;
- hierarchical;
- evidentiary;
- uncertainty;
- scope;
- association;
- counterargument;
- interpretation-to-finding relations.

Rearranging sentences is permitted.

Changing the relations represented by those sentences is not.

---

## 12. Emergent Presentation Insight

BASE may make an implicit relationship explicit when that relationship is logically recoverable from existing BSI material.

Such an insight is a presentation-level synthesis, not a new analytical finding.

BASE must not introduce:

- new causal mechanisms;
- new explanations;
- new predictions;
- new theoretical claims;
- new evidence;
- or new conclusions requiring independent analysis.

---

## 13. Escalation Boundary

If BASE encounters a requirement that cannot be satisfied through organization, synthesis, clarification, or exposition of existing BSI material, the requirement must escalate to BSI.

The escalation path is:

BASE
→ Analytical insufficiency
→ BSI
→ Updated Completed BSI Analysis
→ BASE resumes

BASE must not solve an analytical insufficiency by silently performing new analysis.

---

## 14. No Hidden Upgrade

BASE must never silently transform the epistemic status of a statement.

The following transformations are prohibited:

- possible → established;
- suggestive → demonstrated;
- associated → causal;
- limited → general;
- uncertain → certain;
- hypothesis → finding;
- interpretation → observation;
- correlation → mechanism.

Improved prose must not produce stronger claims than the underlying analysis supports.

---

## 15. Evidence–Interpretation Boundary

BASE must preserve the distinction between:

- evidence;
- observation;
- analytical finding;
- interpretation;
- implication;
- speculation.

These categories may be integrated into a coherent narrative, but their epistemic roles must remain distinguishable.

---

## 16. Local Qualification

Qualifications should remain close to the claims they qualify whenever this improves epistemic clarity.

Important limitations must not be systematically moved into a distant final caveat section in a way that makes earlier claims appear stronger than they are.

---

## 17. Counterpoint Preservation

Competing interpretations, contradictions, unresolved conflicts, and relevant counterevidence must remain visible.

BASE may organize competing positions more clearly.

BASE may not remove a counterpoint merely because it complicates the narrative.

---

## 18. Narrative Architecture

BASE should follow analytical logic rather than automatically following:

- source chronology;
- article section order;
- discovery order;
- or the order in which findings appeared during analysis.

The narrative should expose the structure of the completed analysis.

---

**Structural Implication Guard:** Any structural implication presented by BASE must already be present in, or be a logically equivalent re-expression of, the completed BSI analysis. BASE may expose such an implication but may not generate a new one.

## 19. Analytical Unit

The conceptual analytical unit may be represented as:

Finding → Basis → Interpretation → Structural Implication

This is a conceptual model, not a rigid formatting requirement.

BASE may combine, separate, or reorder these elements when doing so improves analytical visibility without damaging provenance or epistemic boundaries.

---

## 20. Two-Level Presentation

Where appropriate, BASE should maintain two presentation levels:

### Main Analytical Narrative

A coherent, readable representation of the central analytical structure.

### Supporting / Trace Layer

Supporting evidence, qualifications, provenance, conflicts, methodological details, and other information necessary for deeper verification.

The trace layer must remain available even when the main narrative is compressed.

---

## 21. Reading Optimization

BASE optimizes reader effort.

The objective is:

Reduce the effort required to understand the analysis.

The objective is not:

Reduce the analytical effort required to understand the subject.

BASE should therefore improve:

- navigability;
- hierarchy recognition;
- relation recognition;
- qualification visibility;
- provenance recovery;
- conceptual continuity;
- and analytical coherence.

---

## 22. Analytical Narrative

A BASE narrative should answer, in an analytically coherent sequence:

1. What is the central structure?
2. What are its major components?
3. How are those components related?
4. What evidence or analytical findings support them?
5. What interpretations follow?
6. What qualifications constrain those interpretations?
7. What competing explanations or unresolved issues remain?
8. What broader structural implications follow within the established analytical boundaries?

---

## 23. Non-Lossy Presentation

BASE presentation should be non-lossy with respect to materially relevant analytical information.

Compression is acceptable when it preserves:

- meaning;
- relations;
- uncertainty;
- scope;
- provenance;
- counterpoints;
- and epistemic status.

---

## 24. Presentation Integrity

Presentation integrity means fidelity between the completed BSI analysis and its BASE representation.

The question is not:

“Is the BASE narrative correct independently?”

The question is:

“Does the BASE narrative faithfully expose the completed BSI analysis without introducing analytical distortion?”

---

## 25. Weight Preservation

BASE does not recalculate, reinterpret, or rebalance BSI weights.

If BSI has weighted analytical dimensions, their meaning and relative structure must remain unchanged.

BASE may emphasize a finding for readability.

Such emphasis must not be presented as a change in BSI weighting.

---

## 26. Failure Modes

BASE must actively guard against:

- analytical omission;
- provenance loss;
- epistemic upgrading;
- uncertainty deletion;
- scope expansion;
- counterpoint deletion;
- causal inflation;
- chronology-driven distortion;
- hierarchy-induced deletion;
- narrative overcompression;
- hidden new analysis;
- interpretation being presented as finding;
- presentation emphasis being mistaken for analytical weighting.

---

## 27. Analytical Creep

Analytical creep occurs when BASE gradually begins performing analytical work that belongs to BSI.

Examples include:

- resolving an unresolved causal question;
- generating a new explanatory mechanism;
- inferring a new theoretical relationship;
- extending a finding beyond its established scope;
- independently validating or rejecting a BSI finding.

When analytical creep is detected, execution must escalate to BSI.

---

## 28. BASE Quality Model

A high-quality BASE presentation should maximize:

- analytical visibility;
- structural coherence;
- provenance recoverability;
- relational fidelity;
- epistemic calibration;
- qualification visibility;
- counterpoint preservation;
- reading efficiency.

These qualities are subordinate to analytical fidelity.

Readability cannot override epistemic integrity.

---

## 29. Final Architectural Invariants

1. BASE operates only on completed BSI analysis.
2. BASE does not redefine BSI.
3. BASE does not alter BSI criteria or weights.
4. BASE does not perform BSI scoring.
5. BASE does not independently validate BSI correctness.
6. BASE preserves analytical provenance.
7. BASE preserves materially relevant relations.
8. BASE preserves uncertainty.
9. BASE preserves scope.
10. BASE preserves counterpoints.
11. BASE cannot silently introduce new analytical inference.
12. New analytical requirements trigger escalation to BSI.
13. Readability cannot override epistemic fidelity.
14. Hierarchy must not become deletion.
15. Compression must not become epistemic loss.

---

## 30. Final Principle

BASE can change how the analysis is seen.

BASE cannot change what the analysis means.

خوانایی تابع تحلیل است، نه تحلیل تابع خوانایی.

---

## 31. Compact Definition

BASE is the structured exposition layer that makes completed BSI analysis visible, hierarchical, traceable, relationally coherent, and readable without changing its analytical or epistemic meaning.
